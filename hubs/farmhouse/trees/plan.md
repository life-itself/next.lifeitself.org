> [!note] This is a plan
> Most of this is proposed, not decided. It will change once we have marked out the ground and talked to the nursery. Last updated 25 November 2026.

Part of [fruit tree planting at the Farmhouse](/hubs/farmhouse/trees).

## How many

About **20 trees and 4 shrubs** this winter, in one or two groups. We hold the rest of the 2025 plan (68 trees) until winter 2027/28, once we know what survives.

Why small: a big planting could be lost to deer and a dry summer while nobody is on hand to tend it; planting nothing means losing another year.

## What

Plums lead, because they do best here. Then apples and walnuts, with a few other fruits that cope with dry summers and lime.

| How many | Tree | Variety |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | Plum | Prune d'Agen (×2), Reine Claude d'Oullins, Reine Claude de Bavay, Damassine noire, Sainte Catherine, Longue d'or de Leycot |
| 4 | Apple | Court pendu gris de Dordogne, Boulonneix, Reinette de Brive, Suzette |
| 1 | Pear | one suited to lime and dry soil (the nursery's choice) |
| 1 | Quince | Cydora robusta |
| 1 | Medlar | Kurpfalzmispel |
| 2 | Fig | Ronde de Bordeaux, Madeleine blanche |
| 2 | Walnut | local seedlings, two years old |
| 1 | Persimmon | Tam-Kam |
| 1 | Mulberry | white mulberry |

Support shrubs: two autumn olives (*Elaeagnus umbellata*) and two goumis (*Elaeagnus multiflora*). They fix nitrogen, shelter the young trees and add a second barrier against deer.

Choices behind the list:

* **French and local varieties**, not the UK list from 2025, because they suit the climate.
* **Vigorous seedling rootstocks** (franc for apple and pear, myrobolan for plum), which root deeper and cope better with drought and little care.
* **Young bare-root trees**, one or two years old. Small trees settle in faster and need less watering.
* **Only one pear**, because pears dislike lime and dry soil.
* **No almonds or sweet chestnuts**, which do badly on this soil.

## Where we buy

From [Le Goût des Arbres](https://www.legoutdesarbres.fr/), a small organic nursery in Lalinde. We plan to order this week and collect at the Lalinde tree market on Saturday 28 November (date to be confirmed with the nursery). ## Where

On the upper field, near the house and the water. Exactly where is still open: we will look at the ground first (see below).

* **Groups of 5–6 trees** on the contour, 5–6 m apart within a group, not long rows. Groups are cheaper to protect from deer.
* **Walnuts at least 20 m from the apples and the pear.** Walnut roots release juglone, which harms them. Plums, quince and figs can be closer.

Before we place the trees, four things to observe: where water runs after rain, where the deer walk, where a water tank can reach, and the contour near the house.

## How we plant

* **No big earthworks now.** A hand-dug half-moon lip below each tree to catch water. A proper swale can come in late summer or autumn 2027.
* **Simple holes.** A wide, shallow hole, nothing added, watered in well. Manure on top as mulch.
* **Mulch.** Cardboard and 10–15 cm of wood chip over a cleared circle 1 m across, with a small basin. Chips from local tree surgeons; cardboard from shops.
* **Deer.** A 1.8 m mesh cage around each tree, or a fence around each group.
* **Ground between trees.** Sow clover.

## Care through winter and spring

The main risks over winter and spring are deer and storms, not drought. The dry months are June to August.

* **Monthly check** by whoever is on site: cages, stakes and mulch.
* **Water in April and May only if it is dry.**
* **From June, in year one:** about 20 litres per tree every 10–14 days, from a tank on a trailer.
