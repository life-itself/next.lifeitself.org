What we know about the soil on the upper field at the Farmhouse. Part of the [Farmhouse knowledge base](/hubs/farmhouse/kb).

A lab analysis of the upper field was done in September 2020, from samples taken at 20 cm depth. The lower field has not been tested.

Texture: clay 29.9 %, fine silt 21.8 %, coarse silt 25.0 %, fine sand 9.3 %, coarse sand 10.7 %. On the texture triangle this is a sandy clay loam to clay loam. Structural stability and resistance to compaction are rated favourable.

## What it means for planting (our first reading)

* **Alkaline but not chalky.** pH 7.6, with very little free limestone (0.3 %). The risk of lime-induced chlorosis, the main worry for walnuts on limestone, looks lower than we first thought. The soil map calls the upper field a calcosol, which fits: decarbonated soil over limestone.
* **Zinc and boron are low.** Walnuts are sensitive to a lack of zinc, and pears and apples to a lack of boron. We plan to add them later if the leaves show a shortage.
* **Rich in potassium and magnesium, good phosphorus.** No general fertiliser needed. Compost or manure for structure and soil life is enough.
* **A good, workable loam** that holds water well. Fine for most fruit trees, walnuts included.

## Other readings

In November 2024 a home pH meter gave 6.4 in one spot, well below the lab's 7.6. Cheap meters are unreliable and it was only one spot, so we treat the lab figure as the reference. A few more readings around the field would settle it.
